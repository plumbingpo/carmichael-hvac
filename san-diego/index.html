Keeping Your San Diego HVAC System Running Longer

Many mid-season breakdowns trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a central air conditioner typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the first heat wave of summer.

Minor issues rarely stay small for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a miserable night.
